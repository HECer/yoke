# Review the product decision

Read the plan, intended users, current product behavior, constraints, and available outcome evidence. Preserve accepted scope unless the user asks to reconsider it. Do not change code during a review-only request.

Answer the questions that determine the decision:

- What user problem and observable outcome justify this work?
- What already solves part of it? What is the smallest complete improvement?
- Which assumptions lack evidence, and what inexpensive experiment could resolve them?
- What are the alternatives, including doing nothing, and their maintenance or operating costs?
- What can fail for users, how will they recover, and how can success be measured?

Separate required work from optional expansion and explain the tradeoff. Do not invent market facts, adoption, team productivity, or future model capabilities. Research time-sensitive claims and cite primary evidence. Mark estimates and assumptions.

Recommend keep/change/remove decisions with a reason, priority, and acceptance measure. Use a diagram only for a relationship that needs one. Ask about a decision only when its answer changes material scope or an irreversible commitment; batch related questions and respect decisions already made. Avoid an eleven-section ritual, automatic outside reviewers, and new dashboards that have no user need.
